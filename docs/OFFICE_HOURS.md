# Office Hours: LiftLoop

Generated 2026-09-23 · Startup diagnostic · pre-product

## Position

The broad pitch, “an ad-swap network for indie apps,” is too marketplace-heavy. The defensible first product is a **reciprocal promotion planner and ledger for small subscription apps that already have 500–25,000 monthly active users**. It must make one painful question inspectable: *am I giving another app more qualified exposure than I receive?*

## Six forcing questions

1. **Demand reality:** no direct customer evidence is available yet. The source supplies market pressure—roughly 2,000 to nearly 15,000 new subscription apps per month in under three years, Product Hunt submissions projected to rise 5×, and an illustrative $6 average install cost—but none proves willingness to pay. The first demand gate is three founders agreeing to exchange real placements and share conversion data.
2. **Status quo:** founders trade launch-day mentions in private communities, buy ads, insert hand-curated “more apps” cards, or do nothing. Tracking is link-level at best; there is no shared impression ledger, fit scoring, or make-good when delivery is unbalanced.
3. **Desperate specificity:** the initial user is a bootstrapped iOS or web subscription-app founder with 500–25,000 MAU, a working onboarding funnel, less than $2,000/month for acquisition, and a need to find the next 100 activated users without paying broad-network CAC.
4. **Narrowest paid wedge:** enter two apps, their audiences, available impressions, and activation rates; receive a transparent reciprocal allocation plus partner-fit risks and an embeddable campaign config. Charge $29/month only after a founder runs a second exchange.
5. **Observation plan:** watch five founders set up a swap without coaching. Record where they invent audience labels, distrust the fairness score, or ask for controls. The key surprise to seek is whether “fair” means impressions, clicks, activations, or revenue—not what the planner assumes.
6. **Future-fit:** as app creation accelerates and paid discovery gets noisier, credible first-party distribution becomes more valuable. The product becomes less valuable if OS/app-store policy blocks cross-promotion or if large portfolios internalize all distribution.

## Premises

- The real competitor is informal founder-to-founder coordination, not an ad network.
- A two-sided open marketplace is premature; curated reciprocal pairs avoid cold-start spam.
- Founders need auditable math before automation.
- Exchange value should be capacity-adjusted and activation-weighted, never a black-box AI score.

## Approaches considered

### A. Campaign calculator (minimal viable)
Single-page planner that computes balanced allocations and exports config. **Effort:** S. **Risk:** Low. Fast learning, no marketplace cold start; cannot prove real delivery.

### B. Curated cohort ledger (recommended)
Planner plus invitation-only cohorts, signed impression/click events, and make-goods. **Effort:** M. **Risk:** Medium. Produces trust data and real behavior while keeping supply quality controlled.

### C. Open exchange (ideal architecture)
Automated matching, auctions, fraud detection, SDKs, and settlement. **Effort:** XL. **Risk:** High. Large upside, but cold start, abuse, privacy, and policy risk arrive immediately.

## Assignment

Recruit three non-competing founders with adjacent audiences and run one manually reconciled 7-day swap. Do not build an open marketplace until at least two pairs repeat.
